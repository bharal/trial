02 Attachment Theory
Be honest: what's your love style when things get serious?
Hazan & Shaver (1987) found that how we attached to caregivers as children shapes how we love as adults — secure, anxious, or avoidant. Spoiler: secure + secure = the good stuff.
03 Love Languages
You're crazy about someone. What do you do about it?
Chapman's Love Language model shows that how you give love often differs from how you need to receive it — and mismatched styles lead to feeling unloved even in happy relationships. Worth knowing upfront.
04 Values Alignment
Where does faith or spirituality sit in your life?
Lichter & Carmalt (2009) found that shared spiritual values predict marital stability more strongly than shared income, education, or even personality — so this one matters more than you might expect.
05 Conflict Resolution
You two have hit a bump. Your instinct is to…
Gottman spent 40 years studying couples and found that it's not conflict that kills relationships — it's contempt and stonewalling. How you fight is basically your relationship's credit score.
06 Lifestyle Compatibility
Career ambition: where are you on the scale right now?
Becker & Moen (1999) showed that mismatched ambition — particularly around hours, relocation, and travel — quietly becomes one of the biggest sources of friction for professional couples in their 30s and 40s.

07 Family & Future
The kids question. Let's get it out of the way.
This one's non-negotiable territory. Relationship science is pretty clear: differing intentions around children are one of the few mismatches where compromise rarely leads to long-term happiness. Be honest here — it matters.
08 Emotional Intelligence
Your person is having a rough time. You instinctively…
Maisel & Gable (2009) found that how people respond to a partner's vulnerability — not just the good news — is what actually determines whether someone feels truly known and loved.
09 Openness to Experience
How much of a "yes, and?" person are you in life?
Openness to Experience (Big Five) predicts whether two people grow in the same direction over time. High-openness and low-openness partners often look like different species by year five.
